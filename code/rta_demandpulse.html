DemandPulse

RTA Future Move Hackathon - Team 7 - bus demand forecast, capacity and reliability flags, cost-neutral swap scenario

Data period: ACTUAL observations for one day, Monday 15 Dec 2025 (Nol card taps and bus running times). GTFS is the planned Sep-Dec 2026 timetable and is not compared with 2025 as the same period. No weekly, seasonal or event claims are made.

1. Forecast: can we see route demand 15 and 60 minutes ahead?

Boardings per route and 15-minute slot, 117 routes (88.5% of boardings). Models trained on 02:00-13:59 and tested on 14:00-23:59 (never seen). Baseline = the last value known at forecast time (15 or 60 minutes earlier). Blue = model, grey = baseline; MAE in boardings per route-slot (lower is better).

Ridge regression chosen by lowest test MAE (15 min: Ridge 16.72, Random forest 16.96, XGBoost 17.45; 60 min: Ridge 21.39, Random forest 21.96, XGBoost 22.07). Predictions clipped at 0. The 15-minute gain is clear; the 60-minute gain is modest. MAPE is better in 5 of 6 cases, worse off-peak at 60 min (it is unstable at low counts). Errors remain large, so use as decision support, not as exact counts.

2. Where capacity and reliability do not match demand

  • route-hours with high demand pressure (boardings per offered place per hour at least 1.0), carrying only % of boardings.
  • route-hours under-used (at most 0.25) out of route-hours matched to running-time data.
  • route-hours where at least 30% of timed departures ran more than 5 minutes late.

Demand pressure is a screening proxy (boardings divided by seats offered), not on-board load. Boardings are card taps (CKI) only. 166 of 200 routes and 3,001 of 3,727 route-hours match the running-time data; the rest cannot be judged.

3. Cost-neutral bus-swap scenario WHAT-IF, NOT MEASURED

Same buses, same trips, same hour: move a large bus from an under-used route-hour to a high-pressure route-hour that currently has a smaller bus. A donor must stay at or below 0.50 after the swap. No trip is removed.

Only 13 of the 28 high-pressure route-hours fall below 1.0 in the scenario; 15 remain and would need other measures (standby trip) or on-board load data to confirm.

4. Decision: what RTA should change, why, and what difference it could make

  • Change 1 - right-size vehicles by hour, keep every trip. Pilot the swaps listed above on the receiving and donating route-hours.
    Why: 1,262 route-hours run at or below 25% boardings per seat while 28 reach 1.0 or more.
    Measure: demand pressure at the busiest hour of each pilot route, before and after.
  • Change 2 - review running-time buffers on route-hours with at least 30% of departures more than 5 minutes late (761 route-hours; e.g. X13, F27, 88, C01).
    Why: late running erodes passenger service quality.
    Measure: share of departures more than 5 minutes late, per route-hour.
  • Change 3 - use the 15-minute forecast as an early-warning input for standby decisions (18% lower error than the last-slot baseline); treat the 60-minute view as indicative (6% lower).
    Measure: forecast WAPE on live days.

Guardrails: no fare changes, no frequency cuts (network equity), no new infrastructure, driver rest and vehicle types respected. Scenario figures come from one day of data and need on-board load (APC) confirmation before rollout.

5. AI agent layer

Agents explain and run guardrail checks on route summary tables only (no card or driver identifiers). They never compute the numbers; every number above comes from Dataiku recipes.

6. Data handling and limits

  • NOL_ID and Driver_id are excluded from every analytical dataset; source datasets were not modified.
  • One observed day: intraday forecasts only; the test half contains the evening peak but not the morning peak.
  • Fleet capacity 0 treated as unknown; 99.7% of running-time events matched a bus with valid capacity.
  • 6,283 running-time events with an hour outside 0-23 were excluded.
